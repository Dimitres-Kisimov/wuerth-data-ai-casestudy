## 1. Procurement / assortment / cross-sell

- **Public-info problem category.** A catalogue in the millions of articles (public, approximate) means constant decisions about *what to stock and where*, especially in the long tail — capital tied up vs. lost sales. The same transaction data also encodes *what sells together*, which drives cross-sell and assortment bundling.
- **Data/AI approach.** Mixed-integer optimization (MILP) for assortment selection under budget/shelf constraints, benchmarked against a greedy baseline; association-rule mining (Apriori / FP-growth) for basket structure and cross-sell recommendations; **rule redundancy pruning** (closed/maximal itemsets + a minimal non-redundant rule set via confidence improvement), because 254 rules is a screen, not an action list.
- **Demonstrated by.** `revops-optimizer` (assortment MILP), `distributor-intelligence-platform` (MILP vs. greedy), and `market-basket-analysis` (Apriori + FP-growth implemented from scratch, cross-checked for exact equality; `basket/redundancy.py`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Assortment MILP beats greedy on modelled objective; contributes to `revops-optimizer`'s **~€160k/yr** modelled uplift and the platform's **€136,972/yr** modelled uplift. `market-basket-analysis`: **224 frequent itemsets, 254 rules, top lift 2.41, 3 customer segments**, cross-sell recommendations with labelled estimates; **category affinity network** — 14 categories, 27 lift-weighted edges, **3 communities via greedy modularity (weighted modularity 0.58)**, with **4 bridge edges between communities** (strongest electrical–lubricants, lift 1.40) as cross-merchandising candidates; new **rule redundancy pruning** — every rule scored by confidence improvement (Bayardo, Agrawal & Gunopulos 1999) against the best simpler rule with the same consequent: **105 of the 254 rules (41%) are redundant; the 149 kept carry all of the list's information**, every pruned rule names the simpler rule that covers it (a test asserts the coverer is itself kept), and of the 224 itemsets **224 are closed / 126 maximal** — closure does not compress this dataset, the improvement criterion does the pruning work. The honest notes stay: a pruned rule is not *wrong*, merely already said by a simpler one; and on this synthetic data the communities largely rediscover the generator's three archetypes — machinery validation, not a customer discovery; 68 tests.

## 2. Pricing & margin

- **Public-info problem category.** With very high order volume, even small pricing/discount inefficiency compounds into large money; margin discipline matters as much as headline price.
- **Data/AI approach.** Price-elasticity estimation and Lerner-index optimal markups; discount-leakage detection from transaction data; explicit correction for endogeneity in elasticity estimates; and a **per-move robustness gate** — before a price move ships, replay the published Monte-Carlo draws and ask whether the *action* survives, not just whether the total looks good.
- **Demonstrated by.** `revops-optimizer` (elasticity + Lerner pricing; `revops/robustness.py` accept/hold gate), `sales-kpi-analytics` (discount-leakage analysis), and `ml-models-lab` (elasticity endogeneity study).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** `sales-kpi-analytics` surfaces a **€2.6M discount-leakage lever**; `ml-models-lab` corrects elasticity endogeneity bias from **+1.52 to +0.03**, with **profit regret 0.89%**. New **price-move robustness gate** in `revops-optimizer`: the same fixed-seed draws behind the uplift risk band (a test pins the per-draw totals) gate every recommended move on three facts — still carried by the assortment MILP in ≥90% of draws, same move direction in ≥80%, executing the *published* price still positive € at the P10 — verdict on the seeded 256-draw run: **17 of 29 moves ACCEPT (EUR 19,032/yr at baseline), 12 HOLD (EUR 16,187/yr parked)**, and the honest headline is that **the single biggest move (+15% on one SKU, EUR 15,836/yr — 45% of the whole pricing uplift) is HELD** because its SKU survives the assortment re-optimization in only 67% of draws *(synthetic)*. New in `ml-models-lab`: the **ablation study** proves which piece of the elasticity model earns its keep — retrained with the demand-shock control knocked out, RMSE explodes **+1.446 (0.129 → 1.575)**, so **endogeneity handling, not estimator polish, is the load-bearing piece** (shrinkage refines only a further +0.031); every variant retrains through the model's own pipeline entry point, and `tests/test_ablation.py` fails if the committed table drifts *(synthetic)*. New **scenario compare with an exact € bridge** in `revops-optimizer` (`revops/compare.py`): two plans are solved through the **same** core and every euro of the difference is attributed to a **named driver**, summed in *integer cents* so it reconciles by construction — baseline **€159,966.19** vs *Cost inflation +8%* **€139,487.29** closes with a **residual of exactly €0.00**, and the single −€20,479 delta hides both halves: pricing *gains* **€7,382.88** from changed conditions at frozen prices while the **MILP-vs-greedy assortment gap loses €23,895.20** *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** The compare bridge discloses its own construction: **two of its drivers are *ordered***, so splitting a shared SKU's Δ into "the world moved" and "we moved the price" closes to the cent but would hand the interaction term to the other driver under a different order, and the assortment gap **ships as one line** because a difference of two optimization objectives over different subsets "genuinely does not decompose per SKU". The gate's verdicts are a **screening discipline on synthetic data under illustrative planning ranges, not a guarantee** — "accepted" means robust under the modelled uncertainty, nothing more, and the 90% / 80% / P10>0 thresholds are stated policy choices, not fitted quantities; the repo says so.

## 3. Inventory / replenishment

- **Public-info problem category.** ORSY-style shelf/bin systems and multi-channel fulfilment need the right stock at the right place — a service-level-vs-holding-cost trade-off at scale.
- **Data/AI approach.** Newsvendor inventory optimization + demand forecasting to drive replenishment points; a **continuous-review (ROP, EOQ) policy** that turns ABC-XYZ service targets into how much to hold and when to re-order; multi-echelon safety stock with risk pooling for where to hold buffer; an explicit **service-level frontier** for what each extra service point costs; and a **supplier-reliability scorecard** that measures the PO receipt history — the discipline a purchasing desk actually uses: measure the receipts, not the vendor master — and prices what lead-time reality costs in safety stock.
- **Demonstrated by.** `revops-optimizer` (newsvendor), the forecasting engines in `sales-kpi-analytics` / `distributor-intelligence-platform` / `ml-models-lab`, `dip/inventory.py` + `dip/reliability.py` in `distributor-intelligence-platform` (continuous-review policy + supplier scorecards), and `supply-network-opt` (safety stock + `supplynet/service_frontier.py`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Forecasting at **MASE 0.38** (9 rolling folds) in `distributor-intelligence-platform`; `ml-models-lab`'s global forecaster at **MASE 0.987 / RMSSE 0.948**, beating seasonal-naive (1.080 / 1.062) and Holt-Winters (1.101 / 1.019); the continuous-review policy on the 200-SKU seed holds **EUR 127,421 inventory working capital** (cycle EUR 111,139 + safety EUR 16,282 — **cycle + safety equals the total exactly, asserted by tests**) at **5.5x turns**, 66 days of cover and a **99.9% demand-weighted fill rate** at the ABC-XYZ matrix targets (A/X 98%, C/Z 88%). New **supplier lead-time reliability**: over 10 suppliers and 2,400 seeded PO receipts (**74.3% on-time** at a stated 2-day grace window), re-deriving safety stock with the textbook variable lead-time formula at the same service targets prices lead-time reality at **EUR 18,626 vs the quoted-basis EUR 16,282 — +EUR 2,344 (+14.4%)**, split exactly into delay (EUR 576) + variability (EUR 1,768): **the wobble, not the lateness, is three quarters of the bill**, and one supplier that delivers *early* on average still costs EUR 542 in buffer — the quoted-basis column reproduces the inventory engine's figure exactly, SKU by SKU, asserted by tests. Safety stock with risk pooling **−65.7%** (network) / **−80.1%** (centralized) in `supply-network-opt`, whose **service-level frontier** prices the trade-off: 97.5% → 99.0% costs about **$14,750/yr per service point — ~6.3x** the first increment ($2,353 for 80% → 85%) *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** The policy's demand is **modelled as normal and serially independent** and its carrying rate / order cost are stated planning assumptions — "modelled, not measured", per the repo; the frontier's dollar figures use an illustrative unit value and carrying rate, not certified rates. The supplier scorecard's receipts are **seeded synthetic data** (it recovers the generator's own hidden profiles — a validation of the machinery, not a claim about any real vendor), 12 receipts per SKU is a small sample, and the +14.4% is a working-capital consequence deliberately kept **out** of the platform's uplift total.

- **Demonstrated by.** `sales-kpi-analytics` (KPIs, forecasting, SQL, exec PDF, **pacing & target attainment**, and now the **rep performance decomposition** in `repperf.py`) and `revops-optimizer` (Power BI / DAX pack + exec deck + the `kpi_uplift_risk` Monte-Carlo risk table). `retail-analytics-real` is the **completed real-data counterpart** (UCI Online Retail II, 1,067,371 genuinely messy real rows, 2009–2011, CC BY 4.0): documented cleaning pipeline, RFM, returns analysis, **lifecycle segmentation**, leakage-safe rolling-origin forecasting — published with measured results, 113 tests on a committed real-data fixture, QA'd figures, exec PDF + Excel deliverables.
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Rolling-origin CV with **MASE < 1 vs seasonal-naive**; a 3-page Power BI report pack + DAX measures; exec review PDF. New **pacing & target attainment** (Stephen-Few bullet chart, euro labels asserted equal to the computed figures): the seasonal-naive run-rate projects **€10.91M — 95.7% of the assumed €11.40M plan** with an **empirical 80% prediction interval (€10.77M–€11.05M)** built from the model's own out-of-sample backtest errors — and the closed synthetic year back-checks it: **the realised €11.28M (98.9% of plan) landed just *above* the band**, an honest miss the module reports "rather than quietly widening the band, because a prediction interval that always contains the answer is lying". New **rep performance decomposition**: each rep's gap vs an even-share baseline splits **exactly** into coverage + frequency + mix + **execution** (indirect standardization — actual ÷ mix-adjusted expected); the honest punch is that the **#2 rep by raw revenue has an execution index of 0.9999** (his +€337k gap is book, not selling: mix +€354.5k, frequency +€198.4k, coverage −€215.8k, execution −€127), one rep swings #6 → #11 once adjusted, and across all 12 reps **92% of the league-table dispersion is territory, only 8% execution** — on this synthetic data (orders assigned at random within region) indices *should* cluster near 1.00, and they do: the module working, not failing. New **customer concentration & dependency risk** (`saleskpi/concentration.py`) — the risk sitting *inside* the revenue base, measured "with arithmetic rather than prediction": over the trailing 12 months the book scores **Gini 0.5476** and **HHI 68** (~**148 effective accounts out of 383**; top-10 **15.4%**; **156 accounts, 41%**, needed to make 80%) — "at company level that reads *unconcentrated*, and taken alone it would be reassuring and wrong", because the largest account (**C0393, €395,740.85, 3.51% of revenue**) is **35.76% of one rep's own book**, whose **HHI is 2,064** with a top-3 of **64.10%** and **4.8 effective accounts**: "company-wide the book looks calm … It is not calm inside every territory" *(synthetic)*.
- **Measured result *(real data — `retail-analytics-real`)*.** Cleaning logs every step: **94.0% of rows retained**, **22.77% missing CustomerID** flagged (kept for revenue, excluded from customer analytics), **£19,643,862** revenue analyzed. New **returns & cancellations** analysis: returns run at **3.65% of gross value** (£716,426, leaving £18,927,436 net) and 4.18% of units across **17,914 return lines in 7,405 cancellation invoices**; with no credit-note → invoice key in the data, attribution is a labelled **heuristic** (most recent prior same-SKU, same-customer sale): **95.0% of returned value matches a prior purchase at a median 10-day lag** — "a heuristic attribution, not a linked RMA". RFM: **5,852 identified customers, 10 segments** — Champions are **25% of customers carrying 69.0% of identified revenue**. New **lifecycle segmentation & growth accounting** (5,824 customers, 24 complete months; the 3-month dormancy threshold audited against the measured gap distribution; the flow matrix's structural zeros asserted by tests): the average month has 1,041 active buyers = 212 new + 390 retained + 439 resurrected (quick ratio 1.05) — **in a typical month resurrections outnumber month-over-month repeats (439 vs 390)**, dormant customers return at **8.9% per month**, and **31.2% of stage-attributed revenue is customers coming back from a gap** — for this wholesale-heavy base skipping months is *normal purchasing behaviour*, and a CRM that treats every one-month absence as churn would misread most of it (stage definitions are a declared lens, not a behavioural truth). Forecasting (5-fold rolling-origin CV, leakage-safe): the honest headline is that **seasonal-naive wins** — MASE **1.094** vs Holt-Winters **1.187** and lag-features **1.590** — with only one seasonal cycle of training data. Reported plainly, not hidden. New **price ladders** *(real data)*: "`Price` in this dataset is not a property of a product — it is a property of a *line*" — **4,342 of 4,895 SKUs (88.7%) sold at more than one price**, so the ladder is measured first. - **Honesty note.** The concentration layer's every "what if we lost them" figure is **removal arithmetic on measured revenue** — **no churn probability is modelled anywhere** and no RFM segment is attached to an account; the module's first cut labelled the largest, still-ordering account "Churned / dormant", and "the honest fix was to drop the label, not to soften it" (a test now asserts no churn/RFM key can ever reappear): "the concentration module refuses to guess at churn, and that cost it a feature". The HHI bands are the **DOJ–FTC merger-guideline cuts borrowed as a yardstick for a customer portfolio — an analogy, not a regulatory reading**. On the real-data side, "posted price" is itself **a documented choice** (modal by lines, ties to the lower rung), "not a price list the retailer published", the two slopes are **kept apart on purpose** (the within-week one is measured on variation containing no price change at all), and the repo states plainly that **prices were never randomised — no experiment, no cost data, no competitor prices, no stock levels**.

## 5. Logistics / routing

- **Public-info problem category.** Deliveries across branches and customers make route efficiency a direct cost lever (km, time, fuel).
- **Data/AI approach.** Capacitated vehicle routing via constraint programming (OR-Tools CP-SAT) vs. heuristic baselines; a **demand-uncertainty robustness stress test** (seeded scenarios + detour-to-depot recourse + a capacity-headroom sweep), because the cheapest plan on paper is not the cheapest plan on the road; and the **Fleet Size and Mix VRP** (Golden, Assad, Levy & Gheysens, 1984) — a typed vehicle pool with per-type capacities, EUR/km *and a fixed cost per deployed van*, so the objective is money, not kilometres, and the question becomes *which combination of van sizes serves today's demand cheapest?*
- **Demonstrated by.** `route-optimizer` (74 tests; `routeopt/robustness.py` + `routeopt/fleetmix.py` + `routeopt/shifts.py`) and the routing engine in `distributor-intelligence-platform`.
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** **4.6% / 31%** savings vs. heuristics in `route-optimizer`; **25% km saved** in the platform routing demo. Robustness: across **200 seeded demand scenarios** (±15% noise — a labelled assumption) the zero-buffer plans hit a capacity failure in **~96% of scenarios**; re-planning with just **5% capacity headroom** costs +3.0% planned km but cuts failing scenarios **96% → 44%** and the *expected* day by **4.6%** (1,119 → 1,067 km) — the flaw-of-averages result, measured; at 15% headroom the failure rate is ~1% for +13.4% planned km *(synthetic; the noise level is "modelled, not measured", per the repo)*. New **fleet mix**, on an illustrative labelled catalogue (capacity 25/50/100 at EUR 40/60/90 fixed + EUR 0.70/1.00/1.35 per km): on the 60-customer instance **consolidation wins outright** — 5 large vans (EUR 1,325/day) undercut the status-quo 10 mediums (EUR 1,606) by **17.5%** — **and the service price is shown, not hidden: the longest route grows +25.2%**; on the 30-customer instance **the mix is genuine** — 1 medium + 2 large (EUR 628/day) beats the best single-size fleet by **8.5%**, because the tail of demand does not justify a third big van. Every mixed-vs-homogeneous comparison runs under the identical cost model with a fixed solution limit (byte-identical reruns); catalogue costs are illustrative estimates, not quotes, every plan is a heuristic under a fixed search budget — on a fixed budget the mixed pool's larger search space can even trail a homogeneous solve, and the deliverable reports whatever the numbers say. New **driver shifts & working time** (`routeopt/shifts.py`) — "a van can drive all day; a **driver** cannot": the day is modelled as driving, service and break minutes under a **600-min duty envelope**, a **540-min daily driving limit** and a mandated **45-min break before 270 min** of continuous driving. The finding is a negative one and it stands as measured: **"The statutory limits are slack on this data."** — worst duty **267 min (4h27)** across 10 vans (**333 min of headroom**), worst continuous drive **227 min** against the 270-min threshold (**43 min of slack**), **zero breaks** required, **10/10 vans** inside both limits. Capping the duty prices the constraint: **480 and 420 min cost nothing at all** (1,001.6 km, 10 vans), **240 min +5.3% distance**, **210 min +17.5% and an eleventh van**, and at **180 min there is no plan at any fleet size** — "a *proof*, not a search that gave up". The defaults are **informed by EU Regulation (EC) No 561/2006 as a modelling choice, not a compliance certification**: "a real duty would be longer than every number it reports", and nothing in it should be used to judge whether a real roster is legal *(synthetic; 74 tests)*.

## 6. E-procurement / order automation

- **Public-info problem category.** E-procurement/EDI and email-based **RFQ → quote → order** flows carry high volume and manual toil; connecting systems and APIs is core.
- **Data/AI approach.** Agentic tool-use loops + **low-code (n8n) workflows** that intake RFQs, call systems/APIs, and orchestrate steps; ROI made explicit. A seeded **reliability benchmark** over those same flows — fault injection, a four-way **failure-mode taxonomy** (success / recovered / detected / **silent**), and **retry economics** — because an agent workflow is only governable once you can *measure* how it fails. New: a **content-verification layer** as the silent-fault countermeasure — the business outcome independently **recomputed from the source input** (no answer key, no LLM; the trust boundary explicit: **trust the tools, distrust the model**), because a fault you can *detect* is a fault retries and reviewers can act on.
- **Demonstrated by.** `agentic-automation-lab` (agent loops + n8n RFQ-intake workflow + `eval/reliability.py` + `eval/verification.py` + `eval/checkpoints.py`; 100 tests), `agent-flow-studio` (visual flow builder + `estimator.js` dry-run cost/latency estimator; 78 node tests), `automation-roi-explorer`.

## 11. Energy management / facility operations *(new)*

- **Public-info problem category.** A group operating warehouses, production companies, and branch sites across 80+ countries (public, approximate) pays industrial electricity tariffs where a monthly **demand charge** is set by the single highest hourly load — a few peak hours per month price the whole month. Forecasting site load and shifting the peak is a direct, recurring cost lever.
- **Data/AI approach.** Day-ahead load forecasting under rolling-origin CV (temperature + calendar regression vs seasonal-naive vs Holt-Winters, MASE-scored), then battery dispatch against the monthly peak formulated as a **linear program**, then — the part deployment actually needs — a **causal backtest** of the whole loop (weekly-refit forecast → month-anchored plan → meter-clamped execution) measured against the perfect-foresight bound; plus the **change-point degree-day decomposition** (the ASHRAE Guideline 14 / PRISM model) answering the analyst's other question — *how much of this load is the building and how much is the weather?* — with hour-of-week demeaning so an industrial schedule cannot masquerade as weather; ROI framed on labelled assumptions rather than a sales pitch.
- **Demonstrated by.** `energy-demand-forecast` (forecasters, the LP, the dispatch backtest and the decomposition written from scratch on numpy/scipy; 72 tests; `edf/temp_sensitivity.py`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Regression at **MASE 0.497 / MAPE 4.8%**, winning **14/14 CV folds** vs seasonal-naive (**1.369 / 17.6%**); the LP cuts the mean monthly peak **368.2 → 291.1 kW (−20.9%)**, worth **~EUR 11,100/yr at an ASSUMED EUR 12/kW-month tariff**, while a fixed evening-timer baseline saves **EUR 0** — on this site, *when* to discharge is the entire product. The causal **dispatch backtest** then answers "and how much survives contact with a real forecast?": the deployable forecast-driven controller captures **EUR 8,066/yr — 72.7% of the LP bound** — with the loss decomposed (forecast error EUR 2,054/yr; the causal one-day horizon EUR 980/yr) *(synthetic)*. New **degree-day decomposition, validated against ground truth** — the point of doing this on synthetic data is that the attribution is checkable: the fit recovers the generator's designed balance points **exactly (cooling 19.0 °C, heating 6.0 °C)** and the heating slope to the digit (3.20 kW/°C), asserted by the test suite; the finding is that **weather-driven load is 68 MWh = 4.5% of the year's energy yet 18% of the July peak hour (76 of 412 kW is chiller load)** — in the worst month the battery is, to first order, shaving the weather. New **battery sizing against the causal controller** (`edf/sizing.py`) — the question procurement actually asks, *what size should we buy?*, and it **refuses to answer from the bound**: every row is a *complete* causal backtest of the same deployable controller re-run against a different battery. At an **ASSUMED linear EUR 375/kWh installed** (the midpoint of the EUR 120,000–180,000 range) break-even over a 15-year life is **EUR 25 per kWh-year**, and **only the smallest system clears it**: 100 kWh / 30 kW earns **EUR 3,328/yr, EUR 33.3 per kWh-year, an 11.3-year payback**, while the default 400 kWh buys **2.4x the saving for four times the battery and an 18.6-year payback** — "the smallest battery on the grid is the best buy, and only it clears the bar". The awkward parts stay in the table: the **600→800 kWh step (EUR 13.2/kWh-yr) is worth *more* than the 400→600 step (EUR 9.4)**, so the curve is not concave; and it is an **energy problem, not a power one** — doubling the inverter to 240 kW buys **EUR 0/yr**, halving it to 60 kW costs **EUR 824/yr** *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** Three losses reported, not hidden: **Holt-Winters loses to the seasonal-naive baseline** (MASE 3.040 vs 1.369) — a real lesson in method-to-problem fit; at an assumed EUR 120,000–180,000 battery cost the demand-charge saving alone is a **10+ year simple payback: the battery does not pay for itself on these assumptions**, and the repo's business case says so instead of inflating the tariff until it does; and **the robust dispatch variant (planning on the 90% conformal band) is a measured negative result that stays in the table** — 65.4% capture while cycling the battery 42% harder, and neither band setting beats the plain point-forecast plan on this site. The decomposition keeps the same discipline: **a modelled attribution, not a sub-meter** — on a real site the split would need sub-metering or an M&V protocol to verify, the occupied/unoccupied distinction is binary while true occupancy is continuous, and the balance points are searched on a 0.5 °C grid; the repo says so. The sizing grid states what it does **not** price: demand charge only (no arbitrage, no TOU stacking), simple undiscounted payback, no degradation, no O&M — and "the per-kWh cost is *linear*, which real quotes are not … it flatters exactly the small systems the table just recommended. **That caveat cuts against my own headline**"; the grid is five capacities and two inverter ratings, not a continuous optimum.

This is the same pre-registered-rule honesty as the `ml-models-lab` anomaly study: the rule picked the simpler method, and that's a feature. The action plan's scope is stated too: one synthetic drift mode (deliberately the one PCA is most fragile to), and the refit window is assumed available, verified clean and free — verification labour, recalibration downtime and the risk of refitting on contaminated frames are uncosted; a green chart over a blind screen is named as the worst monitoring outcome there is, which is why the plan forbids touching the threshold before the measurement system has been checked. The severity layer is labelled just as tightly: "the cut points and the three prices are **illustrative labelled constants**, the grade **mix** is this generator's mix (a real line's comes from its own defect log), and the index is a synthetic-image proxy for 'how much material the mark disturbs' — **not a customer-severity model**. ## 13. Chain integration & reconciliation *(new — the capstone theme)*

- **Public-info problem category.** A distributor at Würth's scale runs forecasting, replenishment, warehousing, transport, and controlling as separate systems. Public and general industry knowledge says the losses concentrate **at the seams**: the forecast quietly uses different numbers than the invoices, and controlling allocates cost over a different order count than the warehouse picked. The integration question — *do the numbers still reconcile after every hand-off?* — is its own discipline, distinct from any single silo's model quality.
- **Data/AI approach.** One provenance-tagged pipeline (`real` | `derived` | `synthetic-assigned`, a derived quantity inheriting the **weakest** provenance of its inputs) through the whole chain, closed by a **reconciliation ledger**: machine-checked identity assertions that print both numbers at every seam, each with a deliberate-corruption FAIL path in the tests. On top of it, an **agentic-integration layer** via the open **Model Context Protocol**, so an AI assistant can call the real engines with honest schemas. The reconciliation now reaches the **order plane** — a per-order cost-to-serve allocation whose conservation is itself a machine-checked identity — and the **operational knob** too: a van-capacity sweep that re-solves every delivery day's routing per setting and proves only the transport line moves. The MCP layer's honesty is **machine-readable** (every tool result carries a contract-required provenance block an agent can branch on), and now **idempotent**: identical requests are served from a contract-visible result cache instead of recomputed.
- **Demonstrated by.** `distributor-intelligence-platform` (`dip/plandiff.py` plan-diff € bridge over 11 change identities), `decision-chain` (committed run artifact; offline CHAIN DASHBOARD; `cost_drivers.py` + `sensitivity.py` + `order_costs.py` + `fleet_sweep.py` chain consumers; byte-reproducible PDF/Excel deliverables; every identity carries a deliberate-corruption FAIL path in the tests) and `chain-mcp` (official `mcp` Python SDK; **193 tests** incl. a live JSON-RPC handshake and an engine-free input-validation matrix; **contract-enforced machine-readable result provenance** on every result, success or error — a canonical data label, the engine repo + commit it was computed from, and an honestly-scoped determinism flag — cross-checked against each tool description's prose so the label and the wording cannot disagree; **idempotency + result caching**: a reproducible SHA-256 idempotency key binding the canonical arguments *and the exact engine checkout* — so a cached result can never outlive the code that computed it — with `provenance.cache` (cacheable / hit / key) required by the contract envelope, cacheability derived from the provenance registry (never-deterministic tools and error results are never cached), a bounded in-process LRU, and the honest scope stated: one server process, no persistence; per-tool `data_note` honesty labels; Claude Desktop / Claude Code configs).
- **Measured result *(real data + labelled synthetic-assigned layers)*.** UCI Online Retail II (**1,067,371 raw rows**, *real*) through ingest → forecast → inventory → warehouse → transport → costing: **all 13 cross-stage identities PASS**, including cleaned revenue reproduced across two repositories **to the penny (GBP 19,643,861.62)**, the ledger's window revenue equal to the cleaned data's to the penny (GBP 1,047,042.41), the ledger total to the cent (253,427.16), and every pick (256,787 lines), carton (70,820) and route drop (4,151) conserved. Four **additive** cross-stage identities extend the suite without touching the committed artifact: **identity (n)** rebuilds the ledger total from the physical drivers the earlier stages booked (253,427.16 == 253,427.16, to the cent); **identity (o) — forecast-error containment** — sweeps a proportional forecast error across a deterministic grid and proves it moves **only the holding line** (`total(f) = base_total + (f − 1) × base_holding`): the elasticity of total cost-to-serve to a forecast error **equals the holding cost share exactly — 0.0580 (5.80%) on the committed full run** — so a forecast error cannot reach the delivered-cost lines of an already-real order book; and **identity (p) — per-order cost-to-serve** — spreads the ledger over the window's **4,151 real orders** by labelled allocation rules (labour by each order's own DES pick minutes, transport by its carton share of its delivery day's CVRP km, facility equally, holding kept honestly on the SKU plane) and reassembles it: every order-plane column back to its ledger line **to the cent**, both planes jointly to the ledger total **to the cent (253,427.16 == 253,427.16)**, per-order REAL revenue to the window revenue **to the penny**, with exactly one economics row per shipped order. The distribution is the finding: the costliest decile (416 orders) carries **59.0% of the delivered cost** (Gini **0.665**; median 20.67 vs mean 57.51 GBP) — a concentration that is now machine-verified rather than trusted. And **identity (q) — the fleet knob**: the van capacity (a labelled synthetic-assigned parameter, base 80 cartons) swept across a deterministic 1–320 grid with **every delivery day's CVRP + Clarke-Wright re-solved at each setting by the existing stage-4b engine**, the ledger re-priced, and all 13 identities re-checked at every knob point — drops and cartons conserved as exact integers (the knob changes *how* cartons ride, never *what* ships), labour / holding / facility / the REAL window revenue invariant **to the cent**, transport == CVRP km × the published rate rebuilt independently, and the total moving by exactly the transport delta; the measured curve shows **capacity buys km only while it binds** (1,686.7 km bought going 40 → 80 cartons vs 194.8 going 160 → 320 on the deterministic fixture path), with Clarke-Wright within **1.2%** of the metaheuristic across the whole dial. The same reconciliation discipline now reaches the *change* between two runs: `distributor-intelligence-platform`'s new **plan-diff station** (`dip/plandiff.py`, `GET`/`POST /api/plan-diff`, dashboard station **ST-07 · Change**) diffs two full plan runs into a **€ bridge in which every euro is attributed to a named cause**, held up by **11 change identities**: on the shipped default pair the headline moves **€136,972.20 → €115,728.86**, and the single **−€21,243.34** delta hides the story — price moves **−€29,931.98** (184 of 200 recommendations moved), SKUs dropped **−€150,919.35** (21 left the range) against greedy re-picks of **+€150,224.01**, routing **+€9,384.00** (32.64 km/run saved), and a publication-rounding line of **−€0.02 against a €4.03 bound over 806 values**. "**The bridge is an identity, not an illustration**": the rounding line is "real, not a plug … never fitted to close the gap", "mis-attribute a single SKU and the bridge stops closing", the **inventory diff is deliberately kept out of the uplift bridge** (a pair changing only replenishment knobs correctly posts a **€0 uplift delta**, tested), and the assortment lever **moves against intuition, visibly**, getting its own line rather than being netted away *(synthetic)*.

Identity (p)'s distribution is read with the same discipline: under the INVENTED rates it describes the **model's shape, never a real order's profitability** — the 191 "model-uncovered" orders (4.6%, modelled cost above their own real revenue) are reported as a property of the labelled allocation rules, not as loss-making customers. Identity (q) keeps one omission deliberate and declared: this ledger prices km, not vehicles — a bigger van is FREE in the cost model, so the sweep's downhill direction is a **model property, never fleet advice**, and vehicle-days are reported UNPRICED so the service trade-off stays visible without smuggling a new invented rate into the committed ledger.

## 14. Fraud & transaction-risk operations *(new)*

- **Public-info problem category.** A multi-channel distributor processes a very high volume of orders and payments (e-commerce, e-procurement/EDI, invoicing). At that volume, transaction-risk screening is an *operations* problem as much as a modelling one: which alerts fire, which of them a small review team actually works, and — critically — how a retrained model is allowed to replace the one in production.
- **Data/AI approach.** Score → calibrate → cost-based alert threshold → review-queue optimization, each step measured against honest baselines (including the synthetic generator's own **oracle ceiling**, not just a random floor); then a **champion/challenger retrain harness** with **swap-set analysis** and **five pre-declared promotion gates**, so a retrain has to *earn* its way into production; and a **selective-labels feedback simulation** — because "the analyst queue doubles as a labelled sample" is only half true, and only a simulation with ground truth can price the other half.
- **Demonstrated by.** `fraud-detection-ops` (from-scratch NumPy; 78 tests; `fdo/challenger.py` + `fdo/feedback.py` + `fdo/reasons.py`; byte-deterministic `figures/champion_challenger.csv`, `challenger_swap.csv`, `feedback_loop.csv`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** On ~60,000 seeded synthetic transactions at ~1.45% fraud prevalence: **PR-AUC 0.270 / ROC-AUC 0.878** vs prevalence-random 0.013 and oracle ceiling 0.367; precision-at-100 **0.40**; the cost-based threshold fires **608 alerts, catches 76/126 frauds at $8,841 modelled cost** vs $20,744 (review nothing) and $72,000 (review everything). Champion/challenger: swap-set shows the challenger swaps out 139 alerts (4 frauds) and swaps in 2 (0 frauds); **all five pre-declared gates pass, verdict PROMOTE** at **$8,632 vs $8,841 — a net $210 (2.4%) modelled win**. New **feedback-loop simulation** (four labelling-policy arms over three retrain rounds, threshold and 100-review capacity frozen): **ranking survives label censoring — final PR-AUC spans just 0.268–0.273 across arms — but the probabilities die**: the assume-legit arm relabels 292 frauds as legitimate, degrades to **3.2x the oracle arm's ECE**, and its alert volume at the frozen threshold starves **651 → 197** (recall 61% → 44% while the oracle arm holds 62–67%) — an alert-starvation spiral whose own dashboard reads **100% observed recall every round, by construction**, because the only fraud it knows about is the fraud it reviewed. New **reason codes with an exact Shapley decomposition** (`fdo/reasons.py`) — "why did this alert fire?", answered "in the shape regulated lending answers it, a short list of **principal reasons**, computed from the model this repo already trained: **no surrogate model, no sampling, no randomness**". Because the champion is linear in its standardized features, fixing a **reference profile** (the mean training-window transaction, scoring **−0.509**) makes the per-feature terms **the** Shapley values under an interventional reference — and "the tests assert that against brute-force enumeration over all **2^m** coalitions, so the claim is checked in CI rather than cited"; the explained set is the **same 608 alerts the shipped threshold fires**. The finding is again a negative one: **"the most common reason is not the most predictive one"** — `merchant_category` is principal on **40.5%** of alerts but confirms fraud only **11.4%** of the time, while `transaction_amount` is principal on **19.1%** and confirms **20.7%**, and heads **59.0%** of the 100-review queue at **33.0%** confirmed fraud. Removing just the single largest contribution drops **88.7%** of alerts back under the threshold *(synthetic; 78 tests)*.
- **Honesty note.** The repo's own reading: **the retrain does not find new fraud** — it catches 4 *fewer* frauds and wins purely by shedding analyst load; the $210 margin is **modelled, not measured**, and rests entirely on the $8/review assumption (price analyst time at $0 and the champion wins); the challenger's ranking is slightly *worse* (0.264 vs 0.270, inside the declared epsilon). The gate exists precisely so that kind of trade has to be argued explicitly. The feedback loop is **a model of a process, not a measurement of one**: review capacity and the 85% chargeback self-report rate are labelled assumptions, chargebacks land at the round boundary (flattering every censored arm), and "ranking survives censoring" is a property of this generator plus a large clean history — not a production promise. The reason codes are read with the same care: contributions are "**logits against a stated reference**, not probabilities, not dollars, not causal — change the reference and every number changes", "'reasons to cross' is arithmetic inside the model, not a claim the transaction would have been legitimate", "**a faithful account of the *score* can still be an incomplete account of the *fraud***", a stability check finds **Spearman 0.9997** on calibrated probabilities yet a **different principal reason on 1.5% of alerts**, and "the four-reason format is borrowed from ECOA / Regulation B adverse-action practice as a discipline: a fraud alert is not a credit denial, and **nothing here is legal advice**".

## 15. Maintenance & asset reliability *(new)*

- **Public-info problem category.** A group with its own production companies, warehouse automation, vehicle fleets and very many installed customer systems (ORSY racks, vending machines — public, approximate) has a recurring maintain-or-replace question: watch the equipment and intervene on condition, or replace on a calendar?
- **Data/AI approach.** Sensor-based anomaly detection ranked against simple baselines; **censored Weibull survival modelling** (maximum likelihood over observed failures *and* right-censored survivors); **age-replacement optimization** from renewal theory; a three-way **policy pricing** — run-to-failure vs calendar age-replacement vs condition-based — so the detector has to beat the calendar on cost, not on excitement; and a **CBM threshold re-optimization** that closes the loop: re-price the condition-based policy at every candidate alert threshold, because the 5%-FPR default is a statistical convention nobody chose economically.
- **Demonstrated by.** `predictive-maintenance` (80 tests; `pdm/policy.py` + `pdm/cbm_tuning.py` + `pdm/spares.py`; `docs/policy_comparison.csv/.svg`, `docs/cbm_tuning.csv/.svg`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Detector: PCA baseline **ROC-AUC 0.937 / PR-AUC 0.926**, 10/10 failing machines detected, mean detection delay 3.4 days (the conv autoencoder loses by −0.017 PR-AUC and PCA is recommended — the same pre-registered-simplicity rule as the vision repo). Weibull fit on 6 observed failures + 14 right-censored suspensions: **shape β 4.81, scale η 73.6 days, MTBF 67.4 days, B10 46.1 days**. Policy pricing per machine-day: run-to-failure 14.84; **age-replacement 7.16 at T\* = 44.4 days (−51.7%)**; condition-based 8.28. New **CBM threshold re-optimization** — an exact sweep re-measuring timeliness and false alarms at every threshold and costing each with the same Weibull MTBF: economically tuned (0.262 → 0.397), CBM's cost rate falls **8.28 → 4.02 per machine-day, under the age-replacement optimum's 7.16 — the three-way recommendation flips to condition-based** (the earlier finding wasn't that CBM loses; it was that CBM at a statistically-chosen threshold loses), with a closed-form robustness number: the **break-even inspection cost is 553.3 units, ~11x the assumed 50**, before the calendar rule wins again. CP-SAT crew scheduling cuts total weighted delay **23.2%** vs FIFO, proven OPTIMAL. New **spare-parts stocking from the same fitted Weibull** (`pdm/spares.py`) — the bill the policy comparison never sent to the storeroom: every policy is a renewal process consuming one part per cycle, so the fit prices each policy's part-demand rate and a **Poisson base stock** says what 20 machines must hold over 365 days at **95% service**. Run-to-failure and condition-based draw **0.0148 parts/machine-day** (108.3 expected, **base stock 126**); **age replacement at T\* = 44.4 d** draws **0.0229** (166.8 expected, **base stock 188**) — the calendar rule that wins on cost **"draws +54% more parts than run-to-failure … while cutting the cost rate 51.7%"**, a trade-off the policy table alone could not show. The Poisson assumption is checked rather than assumed: measured cycle CV **0.24** and **0.07**, "both well under the 1.00 Poisson assumes", and a renewal-variance alternative would hold **112 / 168** instead *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** The original loss stays in the table: **at the default 5%-FPR threshold the calendar rule beats the repo's own detector** — the detector alerts 6/6 failures ≥3 days early, but its false alarms book more than half the condition-based policy's cost, and β ≈ 4.8 (failures clustering around a characteristic life) is exactly the regime where a calendar rule is hard to beat. The flip is honestly bounded: the tuned threshold is selected on the same held-out machine-days it is then priced on (no second holdout), the 6/6 timeliness rests on six observed failures, and the repo calls it "the *shape* of the closed loop, not a certified operating point" — a demonstration that the loop *can* be closed and how, not evidence that CBM wins on a real fleet. The spares plan is bounded the same way: **"The spares plan is a modelled provisioning exercise, not an order."** — the Poisson base stock assumes independent machines drawing from one pool with **no replenishment lead time, no batching and no repairable-part loop**, the renewal-variance correction is *asymptotic*, and "the layer counts units, not money, so it cannot tell you whether holding 188 spares is worth it". Everything is labelled **modelled, not measured**.
