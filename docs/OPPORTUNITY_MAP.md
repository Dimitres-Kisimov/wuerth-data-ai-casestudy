## 1. Procurement / assortment / cross-sell

- **Public-info problem category.** A catalogue in the millions of articles (public, approximate) means constant decisions about *what to stock and where*, especially in the long tail — capital tied up vs. lost sales. The same transaction data also encodes *what sells together*, which drives cross-sell and assortment bundling.
- **Data/AI approach.** Mixed-integer optimization (MILP) for assortment selection under budget/shelf constraints, benchmarked against a greedy baseline; association-rule mining (Apriori / FP-growth) for basket structure and cross-sell recommendations; **rule redundancy pruning** (closed/maximal itemsets + a minimal non-redundant rule set via confidence improvement), because 254 rules is a screen, not an action list.
- **Demonstrated by.** `revops-optimizer` (assortment MILP), `distributor-intelligence-platform` (MILP vs. greedy), and `market-basket-analysis` (Apriori + FP-growth implemented from scratch, cross-checked for exact equality; `basket/redundancy.py`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Assortment MILP beats greedy on modelled objective; contributes to `revops-optimizer`'s **~€160k/yr** modelled uplift and the platform's **€136,972/yr** modelled uplift. `market-basket-analysis`: **224 frequent itemsets, 254 rules, top lift 2.41, 3 customer segments**, cross-sell recommendations with labelled estimates; **category affinity network** — 14 categories, 27 lift-weighted edges, **3 communities via greedy modularity (weighted modularity 0.58)**, with **4 bridge edges between communities** (strongest electrical–lubricants, lift 1.40) as cross-merchandising candidates; new **rule redundancy pruning** — every rule scored by confidence improvement (Bayardo, Agrawal & Gunopulos 1999) against the best simpler rule with the same consequent: **105 of the 254 rules (41%) are redundant; the 149 kept carry all of the list's information**, every pruned rule names the simpler rule that covers it (a test asserts the coverer is itself kept), and of the 224 itemsets **224 are closed / 126 maximal** — closure does not compress this dataset, the improvement criterion does the pruning work. The honest notes stay: a pruned rule is not *wrong*, merely already said by a simpler one; and on this synthetic data the communities largely rediscover the generator's three archetypes — machinery validation, not a customer discovery; 68 tests.

## 2. Pricing & margin

- **Public-info problem category.** With very high order volume, even small pricing/discount inefficiency compounds into large money; margin discipline matters as much as headline price.
- **Data/AI approach.** Price-elasticity estimation and Lerner-index optimal markups; discount-leakage detection from transaction data; explicit correction for endogeneity in elasticity estimates; and a **per-move robustness gate** — before a price move ships, replay the published Monte-Carlo draws and ask whether the *action* survives, not just whether the total looks good.
- **Demonstrated by.** `revops-optimizer` (elasticity + Lerner pricing; `revops/robustness.py` accept/hold gate), `sales-kpi-analytics` (discount-leakage analysis), and `ml-models-lab` (elasticity endogeneity study).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** `sales-kpi-analytics` surfaces a **€2.6M discount-leakage lever**; `ml-models-lab` corrects elasticity endogeneity bias from **+1.52 to +0.03**, with **profit regret 0.89%**. New **price-move robustness gate** in `revops-optimizer`: the same fixed-seed draws behind the uplift risk band (a test pins the per-draw totals) gate every recommended move on three facts — still carried by the assortment MILP in ≥90% of draws, same move direction in ≥80%, executing the *published* price still positive € at the P10 — verdict on the seeded 256-draw run: **17 of 29 moves ACCEPT (EUR 19,032/yr at baseline), 12 HOLD (EUR 16,187/yr parked)**, and the honest headline is that **the single biggest move (+15% on one SKU, EUR 15,836/yr — 45% of the whole pricing uplift) is HELD** because its SKU survives the assortment re-optimization in only 67% of draws *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** The gate's verdicts are a **screening discipline on synthetic data under illustrative planning ranges, not a guarantee** — "accepted" means robust under the modelled uncertainty, nothing more, and the 90% / 80% / P10>0 thresholds are stated policy choices, not fitted quantities; the repo says so.

## 3. Inventory / replenishment

- **Public-info problem category.** ORSY-style shelf/bin systems and multi-channel fulfilment need the right stock at the right place — a service-level-vs-holding-cost trade-off at scale.
- **Data/AI approach.** Newsvendor inventory optimization + demand forecasting to drive replenishment points; a **continuous-review (ROP, EOQ) policy** that turns ABC-XYZ service targets into how much to hold and when to re-order; multi-echelon safety stock with risk pooling for where to hold buffer; an explicit **service-level frontier** for what each extra service point costs; and a **supplier-reliability scorecard** that measures the PO receipt history — the discipline a purchasing desk actually uses: measure the receipts, not the vendor master — and prices what lead-time reality costs in safety stock.
- **Demonstrated by.** `revops-optimizer` (newsvendor), the forecasting engines in `sales-kpi-analytics` / `distributor-intelligence-platform` / `ml-models-lab`, `dip/inventory.py` + `dip/reliability.py` in `distributor-intelligence-platform` (continuous-review policy + supplier scorecards), and `supply-network-opt` (safety stock + `supplynet/service_frontier.py`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Forecasting at **MASE 0.38** (9 rolling folds) in `distributor-intelligence-platform`; `ml-models-lab`'s global forecaster at **MASE 0.987 / RMSSE 0.948**, beating seasonal-naive (1.080 / 1.062) and Holt-Winters (1.101 / 1.019); the continuous-review policy on the 200-SKU seed holds **EUR 127,421 inventory working capital** (cycle EUR 111,139 + safety EUR 16,282 — **cycle + safety equals the total exactly, asserted by tests**) at **5.5x turns**, 66 days of cover and a **99.9% demand-weighted fill rate** at the ABC-XYZ matrix targets (A/X 98%, C/Z 88%). New **supplier lead-time reliability**: over 10 suppliers and 2,400 seeded PO receipts (**74.3% on-time** at a stated 2-day grace window), re-deriving safety stock with the textbook variable lead-time formula at the same service targets prices lead-time reality at **EUR 18,626 vs the quoted-basis EUR 16,282 — +EUR 2,344 (+14.4%)**, split exactly into delay (EUR 576) + variability (EUR 1,768): **the wobble, not the lateness, is three quarters of the bill**, and one supplier that delivers *early* on average still costs EUR 542 in buffer — the quoted-basis column reproduces the inventory engine's figure exactly, SKU by SKU, asserted by tests. Safety stock with risk pooling **−65.7%** (network) / **−80.1%** (centralized) in `supply-network-opt`, whose **service-level frontier** prices the trade-off: 97.5% → 99.0% costs about **$14,750/yr per service point — ~6.3x** the first increment ($2,353 for 80% → 85%) *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** The policy's demand is **modelled as normal and serially independent** and its carrying rate / order cost are stated planning assumptions — "modelled, not measured", per the repo; the frontier's dollar figures use an illustrative unit value and carrying rate, not certified rates. The supplier scorecard's receipts are **seeded synthetic data** (it recovers the generator's own hidden profiles — a validation of the machinery, not a claim about any real vendor), 12 receipts per SKU is a small sample, and the +14.4% is a working-capital consequence deliberately kept **out** of the platform's uplift total.

- **Public-info problem category.** Field sales, branches, and e-commerce generate transaction data that must become decisions branch/field managers can act on.

- **Demonstrated by.** `sales-kpi-analytics` (KPIs, forecasting, SQL, exec PDF, **pacing & target attainment**, and now the **rep performance decomposition** in `repperf.py`) and `revops-optimizer` (Power BI / DAX pack + exec deck + the `kpi_uplift_risk` Monte-Carlo risk table). `retail-analytics-real` is the **completed real-data counterpart** (UCI Online Retail II, 1,067,371 genuinely messy real rows, 2009–2011, CC BY 4.0): documented cleaning pipeline, RFM, returns analysis, **lifecycle segmentation**, leakage-safe rolling-origin forecasting — published with measured results, 113 tests on a committed real-data fixture, QA'd figures, exec PDF + Excel deliverables.
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Rolling-origin CV with **MASE < 1 vs seasonal-naive**; a 3-page Power BI report pack + DAX measures; exec review PDF. New **pacing & target attainment** (Stephen-Few bullet chart, euro labels asserted equal to the computed figures): the seasonal-naive run-rate projects **€10.91M — 95.7% of the assumed €11.40M plan** with an **empirical 80% prediction interval (€10.77M–€11.05M)** built from the model's own out-of-sample backtest errors — and the closed synthetic year back-checks it: **the realised €11.28M (98.9% of plan) landed just *above* the band**, an honest miss the module reports "rather than quietly widening the band, because a prediction interval that always contains the answer is lying". New **rep performance decomposition**: each rep's gap vs an even-share baseline splits **exactly** into coverage + frequency + mix + **execution** (indirect standardization — actual ÷ mix-adjusted expected); the honest punch is that the **#2 rep by raw revenue has an execution index of 0.9999** (his +€337k gap is book, not selling: mix +€354.5k, frequency +€198.4k, coverage −€215.8k, execution −€127), one rep swings #6 → #11 once adjusted, and across all 12 reps **92% of the league-table dispersion is territory, only 8% execution** — on this synthetic data (orders assigned at random within region) indices *should* cluster near 1.00, and they do: the module working, not failing *(synthetic)*.
- **Measured result *(real data — `retail-analytics-real`)*.** Cleaning logs every step: **94.0% of rows retained**, **22.77% missing CustomerID** flagged (kept for revenue, excluded from customer analytics), **£19,643,862** revenue analyzed. Forecasting (5-fold rolling-origin CV, leakage-safe): the honest headline is that **seasonal-naive wins** — MASE **1.094** vs Holt-Winters **1.187** and lag-features **1.590** — with only one seasonal cycle of training data. Reported plainly, not hidden.

## 5. Logistics / routing

- **Public-info problem category.** Deliveries across branches and customers make route efficiency a direct cost lever (km, time, fuel).
- **Data/AI approach.** Capacitated vehicle routing via constraint programming (OR-Tools CP-SAT) vs. heuristic baselines; a **demand-uncertainty robustness stress test** (seeded scenarios + detour-to-depot recourse + a capacity-headroom sweep), because the cheapest plan on paper is not the cheapest plan on the road.
- **Demonstrated by.** `route-optimizer` (36 tests; `routeopt/robustness.py`) and the routing engine in `distributor-intelligence-platform`.
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** **4.6% / 31%** savings vs. heuristics in `route-optimizer`; **25% km saved** in the platform routing demo. Robustness: across **200 seeded demand scenarios** (±15% noise — a labelled assumption) the zero-buffer plans hit a capacity failure in **~96% of scenarios**; re-planning with just **5% capacity headroom** costs +3.0% planned km but cuts failing scenarios **96% → 44%** and the *expected* day by **4.6%** (1,119 → 1,067 km) — the flaw-of-averages result, measured; at 15% headroom the failure rate is ~1% for +13.4% planned km *(synthetic; the noise level is "modelled, not measured", per the repo)*.

## 6. E-procurement / order automation

- **Public-info problem category.** E-procurement/EDI and email-based **RFQ → quote → order** flows carry high volume and manual toil; connecting systems and APIs is core.
- **Data/AI approach.** Agentic tool-use loops + **low-code (n8n) workflows** that intake RFQs, call systems/APIs, and orchestrate steps; ROI made explicit. New: a seeded **reliability benchmark** over those same flows — fault injection, a four-way **failure-mode taxonomy** (success / recovered / detected / **silent**), and **retry economics** — because an agent workflow is only governable once you can *measure* how it fails.
- **Demonstrated by.** `agentic-automation-lab` (agent loops + n8n RFQ-intake workflow + `eval/reliability.py`; 51 tests), `agent-flow-studio` (visual flow builder), `automation-roi-explorer`.
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** `agentic-automation-lab` models **~€625k/yr**; `agent-flow-studio` **~€47k/yr**; `automation-roi-explorer` **€383k/yr net** *(synthetic)*. Reliability, measured: **1,350 seeded trials** (50 per task × 9 offline task fixtures × 3 assumed reliability tiers) — at the anchor tier (30% assumed fault rate) the RFQ flow scores **76.8% success / 13.6% detected / 9.6% silent**; at 2 retries it delivers **88.7% correct for ~$843/yr in tokens** at the business-case volume, but **~11,500 silent-wrong quotes a year — a number no tier in the scenario gets to zero** *(synthetic; assumed fault rates, labelled)*.
- **Honesty note.** The benchmark's headline is a kept negative result: **guards catch control-flow faults (stuck loops, hallucinated tools) while content faults — a wrong-but-real SKU, a dropped line item — fail silently**; retries only help on *detected* failures, and a bigger retry budget slightly *increases* silent-wrong deliveries. The per-tier fault rates are **assumed scenario parameters** anchored to the published ~70% end-to-end reliability figure — **not measured properties of any real model**, and the repo says so. That is exactly why the business case keeps a human reviewing every draft: reliability engineering decides the *review load*, not the token bill.

## 7. Document processing

- **Public-info problem category.** RFQs, invoices, and order documents arrive as semi-structured text/PDF/email and must become structured records.
- **Data/AI approach.** Agentic document extraction (RFQ/invoice → structured fields) behind a confidence gate, backed by a **field-level business-rule validation layer** — the structural check an AP clerk does by eye (arithmetic, required fields, date order, IBAN/VAT format) — and an **extraction-error cost model** that prices every measured gating policy in euros, because precision percentages only become decisions when they meet prices.
- **Demonstrated by.** `doc-extract-agent` (`python -m eval.run_eval` + `python -m eval.run_cost`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Structured extraction pipeline modelling **~€145k/yr** total benefit (of which ~€110k/yr is AP capacity). Measured on the 27-document eval set: the confidence gate alone auto-posts at **70.0% precision** — a field the regex silently drops can't lower the confidence average — and requiring the **7 business rules** as well lifts combined-gate precision to **87.5%** at the same 8-document volume (a tightened threshold alone reaches only 75%). New **cost model** (measured operating points × the business case's modeled rates — €0.40 pre-filled review, €25 per silent error): **auto-posting a document pays only above 98.4% precision** (break-even 1 − 0.40/25), and no measured policy clears that bar credibly — **the confidence gate alone would lose money on this set** (modeled €181,778/yr vs €173,000 fully manual), the validation layer is worth **≈€109,333/yr in this model**, and the recommendation is the unglamorous one: **pre-fill everything, auto-post almost nothing** — ~€149,000/yr of the saving is the 4-minute → 45-second pre-fill, not the skipped confirm; the ~€110k business-case headline sits inside the measured-mix range (€100,556–€152,556/yr) *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** The euros are **modeled, not measured** — the parameters are the business case's synthetic estimates, the 27-document set deliberately over-represents edge cases, and scaling it to 60,000 documents/yr is an illustration, not a forecast; the remaining gate error (a currency mis-inferred from prose) is invisible to arithmetic/format rules, and the layer "checks format and internal consistency, not truth" — the repo says so.

## 9. Warehouse operations / intralogistics *(new)*

- **Public-info problem category.** A distribution group at this scale runs many warehouses and branch stores: layout, slotting, picking strategy, and material flow directly drive pick travel, cycle time, and safety compliance.
- **Data/AI approach.** A warehouse / **WMS digital twin and plant-flow simulator**: layout editing (or keyword-driven generation) + deterministic simulation to compare strategies before touching the physical warehouse; the standard warehouse operation (receiving → put-away → replenishment → picking → packing → shipping) run as a seeded flow with ISO 22400-grounded KPIs and the bottleneck named; slotting as an assignment problem; discrete-event simulation of process variants; explained heuristic suggestions rather than black-box output.
- **Demonstrated by.** `logistics-flow-studio` (WarehouseTwin — **v3.19**: an offline, browser-based warehouse / WMS digital twin and factory/plant-flow simulator, installable as a **PWA**. A transparent, deterministic **generator** builds a full layout from a plant keyword and an **offline natural-language parser** — *not a trained model* — applies plain-language edits (*"include 2 more RGVs in the picking sector"*); it then **simulates the WMS operation** with ISO 22400-grounded KPIs, a **live animated material flow** and a **live KPI dashboard**, adds storage slotting/occupancy/retrieval, automation modelling (AS/RS, shuttle, RGV, AGV, conveyor), a **factory process model & line simulation** with **multi-way proportional-flow routing** (v3.17: declared split/merge ratios, flow conservation verified at every node), a **generator-emitted multi-way archetype** with **RPW line balancing on resolved effective loads** (v3.18), a **fluids steady-state continuous-flow solver** for the process-industry components (v3.19: pipes/tanks/mixers in m³/h, the saturated pipe named as the bottleneck, volume conservation verified at every node), an **editable standards knowledge base**, **23 example scenarios**, a canvas up to 400 × 250 m and a 2.5D view, a one-click **Story Mode** cinematic tour, an **894-element signature plant** exercising all 29 object types, a **user-definable object library** (Siemens Plant-Simulation *UserObjects*-style — define your own object type) — plus the real-world pass: **import your own article/order CSVs** 100% in-browser with row-numbered validation and exact order replay, and a **floor-plan image underlay** with two-point metric calibration so a real hall can be traced onto the 1 m grid; imported data is excluded from share links by default, and it outputs a consolidated **WMS Report** (print/JSON/CSV) and a scoped **IFC4** export) and `logistics-digital-twin` (packing, slotting, discrete-event sim, pick-path routing, order batching).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** One-click layout optimizer **−48.6% pick travel** on the demo layout (pinned + reproducible in the repo's `docs/MEASUREMENTS.md`); A/B strategy comparison shows **ABC 80/20 beats random slotting ~21%** on pick travel; **45 headless logic harnesses** plus a **112/112** browser self-test back the documented behaviour. The factory pass is measured too: the generated "Machining shop with QA split" archetype resolves its declared 60/40 split to hand-computed flows — offered **120 parts/hr**, bottleneck *QA deep test* at 32 s/unit → **112.5 parts/hr**, line efficiency **≈ 88.1%** — with the pure-chain balancer output **byte-identical to the legacy one, harness-pinned**; the fluids demo blends 40+40 m³/h, names the 30 m³/h pipe as the bottleneck and computes the tank **FULL in 96 min** as pure arithmetic, with **volume conservation verified at every node**. From `logistics-digital-twin`: slotting via linear assignment **−44.2% pick travel** with reshuffle break-even **~0.7 days** (golden-zone A-occupancy **25% → 100%**, rendered as the engine's own hand-built SVG layout, byte-identical across re-runs); discrete-event sim modern-vs-legacy **cycle time −76.1%**, **picker travel −66.5%**; container packing FFD + CP-SAT lifts fill **2.0% → 30.2%** (**56 containers saved**; CP-SAT proves the heuristic optimal on the checked instance); new **pick-path routing** — four classic policies (return, s-shape, midpoint, largest-gap) each measured against a brute-force **exact optimum** (the Ratliff–Rosenthal single-block optimum): at this low pick density **return is closest (+3.0% above optimum)**, s-shape is wasteful (+7.1%), and holding the policy fixed **the velocity-optimized layout routes ~46% shorter than the legacy layout** (51.4 → 27.5 m/order) — no heuristic can beat the optimum, verified per order by the tests; and new **order batching** — FIFO, seed and Clarke-Wright **savings** batching walked on the same aisle geometry against the same exact optimum (extended with a Held-Karp dynamic program): on the seeded 101-order shift, **savings batching cuts total pick travel 2,692 → 774 m/shift (−71.3%)** on the optimized layout and −72.3% on legacy — **batching and slotting stack rather than cancel** — and **batching flips the routing recommendation**: at 6.0 pick faces per tour the best executable heuristic moves from return to **largest-gap (+1.2% above optimum)**, the textbook density result measured rather than quoted; under exact routing no batching policy can lose to single-order picking (a theorem, verified per batch), and a 1-tote cart collapses byte-for-byte to the single-order sweep (travel metres only — tote handling, congestion and batching-in-time stated out of scope) *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** The German-standards panel (ISO 22400, ASR A1.8, DIN 15185, EN 15512, EPAL/DIN EN 13698, VDI 2510/3564, DGUV) is framed **"aligned to, NOT a certification"**, and the WMS/KPI/automation models are transparent teaching heuristics, not a discrete-event-simulation engine or a measurement of a real site. With imported data, the UI badges honestly switch between "Data: synthetic demo" and "Data: yours", and what *stays* a model assumption (pallet counts, picker speed, the order stream if none is imported) is stated in the app itself. The factory line sim and the fluids solver are deterministic proportional-flow / steady-state **teaching models — "modelled, not measured": NOT a validated discrete-event simulation, NOT CFD, NOT hydraulics** (no pressure, viscosity, head loss or pump curves), and the repo says so.

## 10. Supply-network design *(new)*

- **Public-info problem category.** ~400+ companies in 80+ countries (public, approximate) implies recurring network questions: which DCs to operate, how flows route through the network, and where safety stock should sit.
- **Data/AI approach.** Capacitated facility-location MILP vs. a greedy baseline; min-cost flow with an independent cross-check; multi-echelon safety stock with risk pooling; an inventory **service-level frontier** that prices each extra point of service; and a **demand-growth capacity plan** — sweep a demand multiplier and re-solve the same MILP both unconstrained and with today's network pinned, so headroom and expansion triggers fall out of one engine.
- **Demonstrated by.** `supply-network-opt` (71 tests; `supplynet/growth.py`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Facility-location MILP opens **3 of 8 DCs** at **−21.2% total cost** vs greedy (**$83,550** on the instance); min-cost flow cross-checked **LP == graph solver to $0.00**; safety stock with risk pooling **−65.7%** (network) / **−80.1%** (centralized); the **service-level frontier** makes diminishing returns explicit — going 97.5% → 99.0% costs about **$14,750/yr per service point, roughly 6.3x** the cost per point of the first increment (80% → 85%, $2,353) — on an illustrative $50/unit value and 25%/yr carrying rate, stated as such. New **demand-growth capacity plan** (1.00x–2.00x in 5% steps): **the lean 3-DC network has only +8.8% growth headroom** (19,451 units committed capacity vs 17,880 demand — the flip side of the leanness the N-1 resilience screen flags), and up to that wall freezing today's network costs nothing extra; **the first response to growth is a reshuffle, not a new DC** (at 1.10x the optimizer swaps one DC and stays at 3), the **4th DC first pays at 1.30x** — exactly where any 3-DC design physically caps out — the 5th at 1.65x, the 6th at 1.95x an *economic* trigger; and two echelons have two ceilings: the DC pool caps at 2.74x but the plant echelon at only 2.10x, so **plants would bind first** *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** Growth is modelled as **uniform** (every zone scales alike — real growth is lumpy and shifts the map), demand is deterministic, and fixed costs / rates are held flat (no scale economies, no inflation); expansion triggers are model-based planning estimates on synthetic data, not forecasts — the repo says so.

## 11. Energy management / facility operations *(new)*

- **Public-info problem category.** A group operating warehouses, production companies, and branch sites across 80+ countries (public, approximate) pays industrial electricity tariffs where a monthly **demand charge** is set by the single highest hourly load — a few peak hours per month price the whole month. Forecasting site load and shifting the peak is a direct, recurring cost lever.
- **Data/AI approach.** Day-ahead load forecasting under rolling-origin CV (temperature + calendar regression vs seasonal-naive vs Holt-Winters, MASE-scored), then battery dispatch against the monthly peak formulated as a **linear program**, then — the part deployment actually needs — a **causal backtest** of the whole loop (weekly-refit forecast → month-anchored plan → meter-clamped execution) measured against the perfect-foresight bound; ROI framed on labelled assumptions rather than a sales pitch.
- **Demonstrated by.** `energy-demand-forecast` (forecasters, the LP and the dispatch backtest written from scratch on numpy/scipy; 59 tests).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Regression at **MASE 0.497 / MAPE 4.8%**, winning **14/14 CV folds** vs seasonal-naive (**1.369 / 17.6%**); the LP cuts the mean monthly peak **368.2 → 291.1 kW (−20.9%)**, worth **~EUR 11,100/yr at an ASSUMED EUR 12/kW-month tariff**, while a fixed evening-timer baseline saves **EUR 0** — on this site, *when* to discharge is the entire product. The causal **dispatch backtest** then answers "and how much survives contact with a real forecast?": the deployable forecast-driven controller captures **EUR 8,066/yr — 72.7% of the LP bound** — with the loss decomposed (forecast error EUR 2,054/yr; the causal one-day horizon EUR 980/yr) *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** Three losses reported, not hidden: **Holt-Winters loses to the seasonal-naive baseline** (MASE 3.040 vs 1.369) — a real lesson in method-to-problem fit; at an assumed EUR 120,000–180,000 battery cost the demand-charge saving alone is a **10+ year simple payback: the battery does not pay for itself on these assumptions**, and the repo's business case says so instead of inflating the tariff until it does; and **the robust dispatch variant (planning on the 90% conformal band) is a measured negative result that stays in the table** — 65.4% capture while cycling the battery 42% harder, and neither band setting beats the plain point-forecast plan on this site.

## 12. Quality inspection *(new)*

- **Public-info problem category.** An assembly/fastening products group lives on product quality — in its own production companies and in incoming-goods QA, surface-defect screening (scratches, pits, texture irregularities) is a classic vision task where the real question is *when a neural network actually earns its keep over simpler methods*.
- **Data/AI approach.** Three anomaly detectors trained on **clean images only** (local statistics, PCA reconstruction, small conv autoencoder), one shared image-level scoring rule fixed before results were seen, and a **pre-registered complexity rule** deciding the recommendation; ROC/PR/IoU metrics implemented from scratch. On top: classic **SPC monitoring** of the deployed screen — a **p-chart** with Phase-I frozen limits under the four **Western Electric run rules** — because a detector in production is a *process*, and processes drift.
- **Demonstrated by.** `quality-anomaly-vision` (45 tests; `qav/spc.py`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Conv autoencoder ROC-AUC **0.779** vs PCA **0.772** vs local statistics 0.687 — the AE's 0.007 lead is inside the pre-registered 0.02 margin, so **PCA reconstruction is the recommendation**: it also wins the screening operating point (**TPR 0.407 vs 0.393 at 5% FPR**) and localization (**mean IoU 0.207**). SPC: the calibration correction is **measured** — the cost-optimal threshold's "zero false rejects" on 150 test images does not survive 1,000 fresh clean parts (**0 → 0.70% flagged**; the test-set zero was measurement resolution); a simulated process shift is caught by the **4-of-5-beyond-1σ** run rule while the naked 3σ rule never fires in the monitored window, and a camera-drift scenario (true defect rate unchanged) still alarms — correctly read as "investigate the measurement system first" *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** The one defect class that splits the field is stated plainly: texture-breaks are hard for every method (best ROC-AUC **0.609**, autoencoder only — if they dominated a real defect mix, the recommendation could legitimately flip), and training the autoencoder longer made it **worse** (0.779 → 0.738 at 30 epochs). This is the same pre-registered-rule honesty as the `ml-models-lab` anomaly study: the rule picked the simpler method, and that's a feature.

## 13. Chain integration & reconciliation *(new — the capstone theme)*

- **Public-info problem category.** A distributor at Würth's scale runs forecasting, replenishment, warehousing, transport, and controlling as separate systems. Public and general industry knowledge says the losses concentrate **at the seams**: the forecast quietly uses different numbers than the invoices, and controlling allocates cost over a different order count than the warehouse picked. The integration question — *do the numbers still reconcile after every hand-off?* — is its own discipline, distinct from any single silo's model quality.
- **Data/AI approach.** One provenance-tagged pipeline (`real` | `derived` | `synthetic-assigned`, a derived quantity inheriting the **weakest** provenance of its inputs) through the whole chain, closed by a **reconciliation ledger**: machine-checked identity assertions that print both numbers at every seam, each with a deliberate-corruption FAIL path in the tests. On top of it, an **agentic-integration layer** via the open **Model Context Protocol**, so an AI assistant can call the real engines with honest schemas. The reconciliation now reaches the **order plane** — a per-order cost-to-serve allocation whose conservation is itself a machine-checked identity — and the MCP layer's honesty is now **machine-readable**: every tool result carries a contract-required provenance block an agent can branch on.
- **Demonstrated by.** `decision-chain` (committed run artifact; offline CHAIN DASHBOARD; `cost_drivers.py` + `sensitivity.py` + `order_costs.py` chain consumers; byte-reproducible PDF/Excel deliverables; every identity carries a deliberate-corruption FAIL path in the tests) and `chain-mcp` (official `mcp` Python SDK; **145 tests** incl. a live JSON-RPC handshake and an engine-free input-validation matrix; **contract-enforced machine-readable result provenance** on every result, success or error — a canonical data label, the engine repo + commit it was computed from, and an honestly-scoped determinism flag — cross-checked against each tool description's prose so the label and the wording cannot disagree; per-tool `data_note` honesty labels; Claude Desktop / Claude Code configs).
- **Measured result *(real data + labelled synthetic-assigned layers)*.** UCI Online Retail II (**1,067,371 raw rows**, *real*) through ingest → forecast → inventory → warehouse → transport → costing: **all 13 cross-stage identities PASS**, including cleaned revenue reproduced across two repositories **to the penny (GBP 19,643,861.62)**, the ledger's window revenue equal to the cleaned data's to the penny (GBP 1,047,042.41), the ledger total to the cent (253,427.16), and every pick (256,787 lines), carton (70,820) and route drop (4,151) conserved. Three **additive** cross-stage identities extend the suite without touching the committed artifact: **identity (n)** rebuilds the ledger total from the physical drivers the earlier stages booked (253,427.16 == 253,427.16, to the cent); **identity (o) — forecast-error containment** — sweeps a proportional forecast error across a deterministic grid and proves it moves **only the holding line** (`total(f) = base_total + (f − 1) × base_holding`): the elasticity of total cost-to-serve to a forecast error **equals the holding cost share exactly — 0.0580 (5.80%) on the committed full run** — so a forecast error cannot reach the delivered-cost lines of an already-real order book; and **identity (p) — per-order cost-to-serve** — spreads the ledger over the window's **4,151 real orders** by labelled allocation rules (labour by each order's own DES pick minutes, transport by its carton share of its delivery day's CVRP km, facility equally, holding kept honestly on the SKU plane) and reassembles it: every order-plane column back to its ledger line **to the cent**, both planes jointly to the ledger total **to the cent (253,427.16 == 253,427.16)**, per-order REAL revenue to the window revenue **to the penny**, with exactly one economics row per shipped order. The distribution is the finding: the costliest decile (416 orders) carries **59.0% of the delivered cost** (Gini **0.665**; median 20.67 vs mean 57.51 GBP) — a concentration that is now machine-verified rather than trusted.
- **Honesty note.** The honest findings are the product: on lumpy demand **nothing beats the one-week naive walk** (MASE 1.782); the exact Hungarian slotting optimum is worth only **−1.6% over classic ABC** — with the rearrangement-inequality math explaining why; OR-Tools CVRP beats the 1964 Clarke-Wright construction by only **−0.2%** on this geography and loses 19 of 48 days; the synthetic picking crew is **18% utilized** (over-provisioned — measured, not tuned away); and every cost rate is **INVENTED and labelled** on its ledger line, so the cost table makes **no profit claims**. Identity (p)'s distribution is read with the same discipline: under the INVENTED rates it describes the **model's shape, never a real order's profitability** — the 191 "model-uncovered" orders (4.6%, modelled cost above their own real revenue) are reported as a property of the labelled allocation rules, not as loss-making customers.

## 14. Fraud & transaction-risk operations *(new)*

- **Public-info problem category.** A multi-channel distributor processes a very high volume of orders and payments (e-commerce, e-procurement/EDI, invoicing). At that volume, transaction-risk screening is an *operations* problem as much as a modelling one: which alerts fire, which of them a small review team actually works, and — critically — how a retrained model is allowed to replace the one in production.
- **Data/AI approach.** Score → calibrate → cost-based alert threshold → review-queue optimization, each step measured against honest baselines (including the synthetic generator's own **oracle ceiling**, not just a random floor); then a **champion/challenger retrain harness** with **swap-set analysis** and **five pre-declared promotion gates**, so a retrain has to *earn* its way into production; and a **selective-labels feedback simulation** — because "the analyst queue doubles as a labelled sample" is only half true, and only a simulation with ground truth can price the other half.
- **Demonstrated by.** `fraud-detection-ops` (from-scratch NumPy; 55 tests; `fdo/challenger.py` + `fdo/feedback.py`; byte-deterministic `figures/champion_challenger.csv`, `challenger_swap.csv`, `feedback_loop.csv`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** On ~60,000 seeded synthetic transactions at ~1.45% fraud prevalence: **PR-AUC 0.270 / ROC-AUC 0.878** vs prevalence-random 0.013 and oracle ceiling 0.367; precision-at-100 **0.40**; the cost-based threshold fires **608 alerts, catches 76/126 frauds at $8,841 modelled cost** vs $20,744 (review nothing) and $72,000 (review everything). Champion/challenger: swap-set shows the challenger swaps out 139 alerts (4 frauds) and swaps in 2 (0 frauds); **all five pre-declared gates pass, verdict PROMOTE** at **$8,632 vs $8,841 — a net $210 (2.4%) modelled win**. New **feedback-loop simulation** (four labelling-policy arms over three retrain rounds, threshold and 100-review capacity frozen): **ranking survives label censoring — final PR-AUC spans just 0.268–0.273 across arms — but the probabilities die**: the assume-legit arm relabels 292 frauds as legitimate, degrades to **3.2x the oracle arm's ECE**, and its alert volume at the frozen threshold starves **651 → 197** (recall 61% → 44% while the oracle arm holds 62–67%) — an alert-starvation spiral whose own dashboard reads **100% observed recall every round, by construction**, because the only fraud it knows about is the fraud it reviewed *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** The repo's own reading: **the retrain does not find new fraud** — it catches 4 *fewer* frauds and wins purely by shedding analyst load; the $210 margin is **modelled, not measured**, and rests entirely on the $8/review assumption (price analyst time at $0 and the champion wins); the challenger's ranking is slightly *worse* (0.264 vs 0.270, inside the declared epsilon). The gate exists precisely so that kind of trade has to be argued explicitly. The feedback loop is **a model of a process, not a measurement of one**: review capacity and the 85% chargeback self-report rate are labelled assumptions, chargebacks land at the round boundary (flattering every censored arm), and "ranking survives censoring" is a property of this generator plus a large clean history — not a production promise.

## 15. Maintenance & asset reliability *(new)*

- **Public-info problem category.** A group with its own production companies, warehouse automation, vehicle fleets and very many installed customer systems (ORSY racks, vending machines — public, approximate) has a recurring maintain-or-replace question: watch the equipment and intervene on condition, or replace on a calendar?
- **Data/AI approach.** Sensor-based anomaly detection ranked against simple baselines; **censored Weibull survival modelling** (maximum likelihood over observed failures *and* right-censored survivors); **age-replacement optimization** from renewal theory; a three-way **policy pricing** — run-to-failure vs calendar age-replacement vs condition-based — so the detector has to beat the calendar on cost, not on excitement; and a **CBM threshold re-optimization** that closes the loop: re-price the condition-based policy at every candidate alert threshold, because the 5%-FPR default is a statistical convention nobody chose economically.
- **Demonstrated by.** `predictive-maintenance` (80 tests; `pdm/policy.py` + `pdm/cbm_tuning.py`; `docs/policy_comparison.csv/.svg`, `docs/cbm_tuning.csv/.svg`).
- **Measured result *(synthetic)*.** Detector: PCA baseline **ROC-AUC 0.937 / PR-AUC 0.926**, 10/10 failing machines detected, mean detection delay 3.4 days (the conv autoencoder loses by −0.017 PR-AUC and PCA is recommended — the same pre-registered-simplicity rule as the vision repo). Weibull fit on 6 observed failures + 14 right-censored suspensions: **shape β 4.81, scale η 73.6 days, MTBF 67.4 days, B10 46.1 days**. Policy pricing per machine-day: run-to-failure 14.84; **age-replacement 7.16 at T\* = 44.4 days (−51.7%)**; condition-based 8.28. New **CBM threshold re-optimization** — an exact sweep re-measuring timeliness and false alarms at every threshold and costing each with the same Weibull MTBF: economically tuned (0.262 → 0.397), CBM's cost rate falls **8.28 → 4.02 per machine-day, under the age-replacement optimum's 7.16 — the three-way recommendation flips to condition-based** (the earlier finding wasn't that CBM loses; it was that CBM at a statistically-chosen threshold loses), with a closed-form robustness number: the **break-even inspection cost is 553.3 units, ~11x the assumed 50**, before the calendar rule wins again. CP-SAT crew scheduling cuts total weighted delay **23.2%** vs FIFO, proven OPTIMAL *(synthetic)*.
- **Honesty note.** The original loss stays in the table: **at the default 5%-FPR threshold the calendar rule beats the repo's own detector** — the detector alerts 6/6 failures ≥3 days early, but its false alarms book more than half the condition-based policy's cost, and β ≈ 4.8 (failures clustering around a characteristic life) is exactly the regime where a calendar rule is hard to beat. The flip is honestly bounded: the tuned threshold is selected on the same held-out machine-days it is then priced on (no second holdout), the 6/6 timeliness rests on six observed failures, and the repo calls it "the *shape* of the closed loop, not a certified operating point" — a demonstration that the loop *can* be closed and how, not evidence that CBM wins on a real fleet. Everything is labelled **modelled, not measured**.
